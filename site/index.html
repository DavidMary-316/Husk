Good to know
  • Husk is free, has no ads, and never sends your files or anything about them anywhere. It all happens on your device.
  • Only the categories switched on under “What to remove” are removed. Anything you leave off stays in the file, and with nothing switched on the file is saved exactly as it was.
  • This removes data stored in the file. It cannot hide what the picture itself shows, such as faces, street signs and reflections.
  • Invisible watermarks and a camera sensor’s noise pattern cannot be removed.
  • File names like IMG_20261114_064109 can reveal a date too, so use neutral names if that matters.
  • Colour profiles can occasionally carry identifying text. Turn that option off if in doubt, though colours may shift slightly.
  • Videos are cleaned without re-encoding, so the picture and sound stay exactly as they were. The software name an encoder writes inside the video stream itself cannot be removed without re-encoding.
  • Rotation and colour profile options apply to photos only, and only when “Other” is switched on. WebM, MKV and AVI videos are not supported yet.
  • iPhone HEIC files are not supported yet. Export them as JPEG first.
  • Offline copy checksum (SHA-256): a75b0d6f635e6d4f37667f1e3910680ff004b0a53b86770c867e208bd857cb69. After downloading husk.html you can check that your copy matches: on Windows run certutil -hashfile husk.html SHA256, on Mac or Linux run shasum -a 256 husk.html. This catches a damaged download, and the same number is the one to compare if a copy ever comes from somewhere else.
  • Social apps often strip metadata on their own, but messaging apps and email usually send the original file.
